How to Choose a Monitor Arm for a Home Office (2026)

The problem: The monitor eats desk space or sits at the wrong height.

How to use this guide

Criteria only: no products are named and no links are included. Check each point against your own space and the product page before you buy.

Types compared

TypeBest forWhat to check
Single armsOne screen, freeing desk space.Check the weight and screen size range.
Dual armsTwo screens side by side.Check the capacity per arm and the desk width.
Gas-spring armsQuick height changes with one hand.Check the weight range; a screen outside it will drift or not hold.
Clamp or grommet mountsAttaching to the desk edge or through a hole.Check desk thickness limits and edge type; hollow or glass tops may not suit a clamp.
Wall mountsClearing the desk completely.Check wall type and hardware; you may need a stud finder.

Buying criteria

  1. Monitor weight and size versus the arm's stated capacity.
  2. The mounting hole pattern on your monitor (check the manual or the back of the screen).
  3. Desk thickness and edge type for clamp mounts.
  4. Adjustability: height, tilt, swivel, rotation.
  5. Cable routing and whether the arm covers the screen's ports.

FAQ

Will it fit my desk edge?

Measure desk thickness and check for a lip, bevel or crossbar underneath, then compare with the arm's stated clamp range.

What is VESA?

It is a standard spacing of four mounting holes on the back of a monitor, such as 75 by 75 or 100 by 100 millimetres. Check yours before buying.

Single or dual?

Choose by how many screens you use and how wide your desk is.
